The second trend is even more strategic: elevating our back-ends. We have no desire to replace SAP or Salesforce: they are robust, standard back-ends. Their interfaces, however, are unfriendly. Optic 2000 was founded in 1969, and we still have a huge number of manual processes, especially on top of SAP: Excel exports, then hours of repetitive manipulation, with a real risk of error. [clic] Today, our business users build user-friendly Streamlit front-ends, connected directly to SAP and Salesforce, and tailored to their processes. We are only at the beginning. But it’s the use we least imagined, and maybe the one that will change the game the most. [clic] A dashboard shows. A data app acts.
